UX/UI CASE STUDY · WEB REDESIGN

Making comprehensive
dentistry easier to explore.

Restructuring Midmark Veterinary’s Comprehensive Dentistry landing page into a clearer, more guided experience—from first introduction to education, equipment discovery, and next steps.

01 / The challenge

A lot to offer.
Not enough direction.

The original landing page introduced dentistry solutions through a long sequence of promotional blocks, category descriptions, and supporting information. Visitors could find relevant material, but the experience offered limited guidance about where to begin or how the pieces fit together.

The design challenge was to create a more intentional path for veterinary audiences—one that explained the comprehensive dentistry approach, connected education and facility planning with equipment, and made useful next steps easier to find.

Design objective
Help visitors understand the approach, choose a relevant pathway, and explore solutions without working through a lengthy undifferentiated page.

03 / Design decisions

Building a more
guided journey.

Rather than treating the page as a single long list, the redesign introduces distinct ways to explore the topic.

01 / ORIENTATION

Four clear entry points

“Explore the Approach,” “Design Your Space,” “Discover Solutions,” and “Train for Excellence” help different visitors find the content relevant to them.

02 / UNDERSTANDING

Explain the approach

A proactive, inclusive, and purposeful framework organizes the message into scannable ideas instead of leaving visitors to piece together the story.

03 / EXPLORATION

Make solutions scannable

A dedicated video moment and a visual equipment grid create clearer routes from education and inspiration to specific products.

05 / Outcome & reflection

A page with a
clearer purpose.

The finished page provides visible pathways into the dentistry approach, expert design support, equipment, and training. The hierarchy makes these choices apparent early, while the supporting sections let visitors continue at their own pace.

This project reinforced how much of UX design is deciding what deserves attention, what belongs together, and what users need to see next—not simply making an existing page look different.

Evidence boundary: The before-and-after captures demonstrate design changes, not measured improvements in engagement or conversion. Those outcomes would require analytics or usability findings.
